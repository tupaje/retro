## Played

Play started in 2014. By 2016 there were about 40 games. A few years on, the cart pile was 60 to 80.

After an early stretch on Mario 64, Pilotwings 64, Shadows of the Empire, and Star Fox 64, the aim became the whole set — 296 titles, his count.

2017: first EverDrive. 2019: sold the physical games, second EverDrive. Early 2021: the Nintendo 64 Anthology, plus a long run of N64 reviews and retrospectives used as prep.

Around 2021, third EverDrive, ordered during the war in Ukraine. The worry was whether the package would arrive.

The official pass started October 2022 with Pilotwings 64 and finished in 2023. Mid-first-game he tried a beat-to-credit rule. After the first sports game he dropped it as a blanket rule: not every title is worth playing to credits. The point of the pass became playing the set.

Eight months to play the library. English Aleck 64, English 64DD, and prototypes were in. Homebrew was not.

Two years off. 2025: other consoles through emulators, starting with Virtual Boy.

## Keepers and once

Taste, first to second: adventure, then racing. Kingdom Hearts is the adventure yardstick, even though it is not on this console.

The four keepers, all 100%: Donkey Kong 64, Super Mario 64, Banjo-Kazooie, Banjo-Tooie.

Played to 100% and still not keepers: Star Fox 64, Shadows of the Empire, Turok.

Rayman 2 The Great Escape is the platform-adventure he calls a hidden gem — the one that looked ahead.

Turok is the shooter that stands out on this set. The world-jumping is what he says still feels ahead of its year. Once, not a keeper; still the landmark.

Racing, the hard beat-to-credit four: F-Zero X, Star Wars Episode I Racer, Extreme-G, Wipeout 64. Extreme-G is the handling note — blink and the line is gone.

Racing that was fun without that same skill floor: Diddy Kong Racing, Mario Kart 64, Mickey's Speedway USA, Top Gear Overdrive.

## Side note

Beat-to-credit was a rule he tried and then narrowed. The 100% list above is the exception log, not the goal of the file.

## Why it matters to the journey

This is where play replaced completion as the measure, and where keeper vs once became the language. Selling the carts belongs to that shift.
